# Data sources and interpretation

Provider integrations reviewed 31 October 2026; offline-mode additions reviewed 1 November 2026. Providers can change availability, coverage and terms; verify them before release or commercial use.

## Forecasts, models and air quality

[Open-Meteo's forecast API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs) supplies the weather series. Its current conditions are model estimates, not direct readings from an instrument beside the user. Times in the data layer are epoch milliseconds; the display uses the forecast location's timezone.

The comparison uses [ECMWF IFS](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/ecmwf-api) and [NOAA GFS](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/gfs-api) products available through Open-Meteo. Differences show model disagreement; two deterministic forecasts do not define a calibrated probability distribution. Values interpolated onto an hourly grid do not acquire finer physical resolution.

[CAMS air-quality output](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/air-quality-api) is also modeled. Grid averages cannot establish the precise exposure of a person at a particular street or indoors. Preserve CAMS and Open-Meteo attribution when reusing these values. Place search uses [Open-Meteo geocoding](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/geocoding-api), based on GeoNames.

[Open-Meteo's terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms) distinguish CC BY 4.0 data licensing from access to its free, non-commercial API. Current request ceilings are under 10,000/day, 5,000/hour and 600/minute. Ads, subscriptions or greater traffic require a suitable service arrangement; free API capacity is not a deployment guarantee.

Data saver uses the same normalization with `forecast_hours=24`, current-condition variables, and no daily series. Full workspace requests the full seven-day forecast and comparison feeds. Missing variables remain missing rather than becoming zero.

## Official alerts

### Location shortcuts and risk context

The [WorldRiskReport 2026](https://www.welthungerhilfe.org/news/publications/detail/worldriskreport-2026) ranks the Philippines, India and Indonesia first, second and third at country level. StormSignal uses Manila, Kolkata and Jakarta as illustrative city shortcuts; it does not claim they are the highest-risk cities. San Francisco is a regular quick location and is not assigned one of these ranks. The index combines national exposure and vulnerability and cannot establish current danger at a selected point. Ranking data: Bündnis Entwicklung Hilft / IFHV, [CC BY 4.0](https://weltrisikobericht.de/worldriskreport/). No live-alert calculation uses this ranking.

### Warning feeds

### Direct local coverage for Manila, Kolkata and Jakarta

- **Manila / Philippines:** reads the [PAGASA CAP Atom feed](https://publicalert.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/feeds/) listed in the [WMO Register of Alerting Authorities](https://alertingauthority.wmo.int/authorities.php?recId=127), then original CAP documents. The feed contains only some PAGASA products and may omit local rainfall/thunderstorm advisories; the regional authority link remains essential.
- **Jakarta / Indonesia:** reads [BMKG's documented English RSS/CAP nowcasts](https://data.bmkg.go.id/peringatan-dini-cuaca/). This is short-term weather coverage, not earthquake/tsunami monitoring. BMKG (Badan Meteorologi, Klimatologi, dan Geofisika) remains credited. The provider documents a 60-request/minute/IP limit; shared 60-second caching, bounded reads and request coalescing reduce traffic but do not certify high-traffic capacity.
- **Kolkata:** reads IMD's [public district-nowcast RSS](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/contents/dist_nowcast_rss.php), documented in its [published API list](https://mausamaudit.imd.gov.in/Forecast/marquee_data/API_doc.pdf). Only exact Kolkata district records are selected. Every retrieved entry is checked; the feed is read up to 1,000 entries and 1 MB, and a longer feed is reported as unavailable. An entry that names no district cannot be checked: it is counted and disclosed beside the Kolkata bulletin that was read, and the source is reported as unavailable when no Kolkata bulletin was read or the entry mentions Kolkata. Only formatting tags and links are removed from bulletin text; a comparison sign such as `<5 mm/hr` or `<a few mm` is kept. This rolling national feed can contain no Kolkata entry even while the district web page has newer information. The newer authenticated JSON endpoints are not used. Publication, weather onset and expiry are kept distinct; a published warning for a future hazard is shown immediately, with the source onset retained in its text. An issue time up to five minutes ahead of the server clock is treated as published, so it never hides the issue it replaces. Severity stays Unknown because this RSS format does not supply a verified CAP severity. The broader SACHET West Bengal feed remains separate regional evidence.

Direct national checks read up to 20 recent original CAP documents, at most four concurrently, within 14 seconds. XML has a 256 KB bound; original boundaries are validated, not approximated, and point matching requires polygons or circles. A BMKG province bulletin with 197 district polygons exposed an overly small parser limit; up to 256 polygons per area are now supported within the unchanged document-byte bound. Bulletins are read most recently published first; a BMKG feed time without a time zone only sets that order. Missing geometry, missing expiry, failed details, unread feed entries, an unknown reading order, feed times without a time zone, matched bulletins that take effect later and empty results are disclosed.

WMO point lookups run alongside the direct national checks. Exact verified direct document/reference keys suppress older indexed copies, and cancellation references from either source suppress the corresponding originals. A current direct document with an area that has no polygon or circle cannot be evaluated at the point, so it does not suppress WMO's area-description/geocode match for that document. Exact issuer/text/area/validity duplicates are collapsed while distinct warnings remain. Source failures are independent, and a source still answering after 17 seconds is reported as unavailable instead of holding back the other source's results; **Source checks** shows retrieval status, issue time and limitations. The combined coverage text names bulletins that take effect later with the earliest such time, because a saved briefing keeps that text but not the source checks. An empty result or successful source response never establishes an all-clear. Historical briefings retain warning text, original links/times and combined coverage limits; the live source-check panel is not a background monitor.

The [U.S. National Weather Service API](https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api) provides active, point-specific U.S. warnings. The app preserves authority text and expiration, excludes cancelled/expired entries, and uses supplied geometry where available. Some warning products have no polygon in the response. NWS times are compared with the device clock, so an effective time gets the same five-minute allowance for a slow device clock as the issue time.

India uses the official [NDMA SACHET CAP feeds](https://sachet.ndma.gov.in/CapFeed), with a fixed mapping for all 36 states/territories published there. State metadata selects the regional RSS feed; up to ten referenced CAP documents are checked, most recently published first, four at a time. Publication times without a time zone still order the entries of one feed. When an entry has no usable publication time, or a feed mixes times with and without a zone, the result says the entries checked may not be the most recent. A check still loading details after 17 seconds (16.75 seconds alongside IMD for Kolkata) keeps the bulletins verified by then and reports the rest as not verified. Warnings are listed most severe first, so the ten-text bound never drops a more severe warning for a newer, less severe one. RSS is a limited rolling history, not a complete list of current city warnings. Original issuing agencies, named areas and validity times are preserved; a current bulletin that names no affected area is still listed and says so. Region-wide selection does not imply every place in that region is affected. Choose a named Indian city with state metadata; Bhubaneswar's shortcut selects the Odisha feed while its forecast remains for Bhubaneswar.

The [SACHET integration guide](https://sachet.ndma.gov.in/docs/Integration_Guide_For_Agencies.pdf) requires ETag/If-None-Match caching and 200/304 handling. The owned server implements this without exposing a general-purpose proxy. Old RSS publication dates are shown as historical; successful HTTP retrieval cannot establish complete current coverage. An RSS time without a time zone is never read as an instant in the server's own zone: the bulletin time is then unknown, which is disclosed, and the time only sets the order in which entries are read. A zone is recognised after the time of day, also when a year, an offset or a comment follows it. When no bulletin in effect is matched, the result says so for the limited feed, never that the city is safe; bulletins that take effect later are disclosed but not listed as current warnings. Direct IMD links remain available for [Kolkata](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/kolkata/), [Mumbai](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/mumbai/), [Odisha](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/bhubaneswar/) and [Chennai](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/chennai/).

Elsewhere, the server consults the [WMO SWIC official source registry](https://severeweather.wmo.int/sources.html) and its point-intersection service, then reads up to six original CAP XML documents. When the index lists more, the number left unchecked is stated. Only registry-listed official operating sources for the selected country are eligible. Registry status is not a health check. CAP polygons/circles take precedence over area descriptions/geocodes; when geometry is absent, exact source-index area/geocode matching is used. Unsupported countries remain explicit.

Manila uses the registry's PAGASA feed; Jakarta uses BMKG. Both remain partial point lookups, not complete national warning monitoring. A bounded WMO lookup returning no matches cannot prove there is no warning. Direct links to [PAGASA's National Capital Region warnings](https://pagasa.dost.gov.ph/regional-forecast/ncrprsd) and [BMKG's Jakarta/Jabodetabek nowcasting page](https://www.bmkg.go.id/peringatan-dini/curah-hujan/nowcasting/31) stay visible on empty or failed checks. San Francisco also links directly to [NWS San Francisco Bay Area](https://www.weather.gov/mtr/).

BMKG publishes [Indonesian and English official CAP nowcasts](https://data.bmkg.go.id/peringatan-dini-cuaca/). SWIC's Indonesian record can link an English copy under the same authority. StormSignal follows only that explicit known alias and checks the same CAP sender, identifier, issue time, status, references, effective/onset/expiry times, hazard level and original areas. Matching preserves the count of every original information block. A mismatch or timeout retains the original-language warning; this feature does not translate or rewrite official instructions. Source attribution remains BMKG and the original authority text.

[WMO's reuse notes](https://severeweather.wmo.int/note.html) allow redistribution with issuing-authority attribution and explain that inaccessible, missing, non-CAP or unmapped feeds can omit warnings. SWIC is broad international coverage, **not every country and every warning**. During verification, an Australian WFS record had timestamps eight hours earlier than its original CAP document. StormSignal uses WFS only to locate candidate documents; issue/effective/expiry values come from CAP. Canonical WMO-hosted copies preserve source text and issuer. Large map outlines are omitted rather than simplified. An outline that crosses 180° longitude is cut on that meridian into parts on each side, keeping every original vertex, so it is drawn where it is rather than around the world; an outline that encircles a pole is omitted.

CAP parsing rejects malformed/oversized XML, document types/entities, unknown CAP namespaces and future publication times. Feeds are read as UTF-8. One that is not valid UTF-8 is read in the encoding the document itself declares, by byte-order mark or XML declaration (for example ISO-8859-1, windows-1252 or UTF-16). An HTTP charset alone is not used, because servers often send a default one, and one that names a different encoding leaves the document unverified. A UTF-8 document with a few damaged bytes is rejected rather than re-read in another encoding, which would garble its text. In a Cancel or Update, information whose expiry is not after its issue and effective times is never current, but the message still counts, so one that ends a warning at its issue time withdraws the original. Any other message with such an interval is rejected and reported as not verified. The deprecated CAP 1.0 certainty "Very Likely" is read as "Likely", as CAP 1.2 specifies. Only Actual/Public Alert or Update messages that are in effect and have known unexpired validity are displayed; matched bulletins that take effect later are disclosed in the coverage note instead. References suppress cancelled originals at once and superseded originals once the Update takes effect. An affected-area list longer than 4,000 characters keeps its leading area names and states how many more the official bulletin names; it is never cut off silently. NWS area lists are shortened the same way. Detail failures, retrieval caps and unavailable coverage stay visible. Cancellation outside a bounded feed can still be missed; verify the original authority. A device coordinate without country metadata needs a named-city selection for this integration.

Full workspace checks supported alerts once per minute while visible and online. Data saver uses deliberate refreshes with no periodic polling; offline mode makes no provider checks. A check older than two minutes is no longer presented as current. The warning gateway states its own clock in each answer, and check ages and validity times are judged on that clock, so a device clock running fast or slow does not hide a fresh check or a warning that has just taken effect. The warning list, the map and page notifications judge expiry on that clock as well: a warning stays listed until its stated expiry and no longer, whatever the device clock reads. When the two clocks differ by five minutes or more, the warning panel says so. Official warning times are shown with their time zone. Historical saved briefings preserve source times and always require current verification. This is a retrieval interval, not a promise that an authority publishes new data every minute. Optional page notifications are limited to an open page and supported browsers; there is no server push-warning subscription.

Forecast-derived hazard indicators are application heuristics. They are not authority-issued alert colors, evacuation advice or warnings. Their thresholds live in `src/risk.ts`; they should remain inspectable and tested. An absence of indicators cannot establish safety.

## Regional disaster reports

The separate regional panel uses the **Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, GDACS**, through its [official API](https://www.gdacs.org/gdacsapi/swagger/index.html). The [GDACS quickstart](https://data.gdacs.org/Documents/2025/GDACS_API_quickstart_v1.pdf) documents free access, attribution and pagination. Review its linked [terms of use](https://www.gdacs.org/documents/2025/GDACS_Terms_of_use_Mar_25.pdf) when reusing the reports.

StormSignal requests recent tropical-cyclone, flood and wildfire reports, including red, orange and green overall impact assessments, from `https://www.gdacs.org/gdacsapi/api/Events/geteventlist/SEARCH`. Retrieval is limited to two pages of 100 global records; the UI discloses capped or partial retrieval. Repeated event episodes are deduplicated. A 14-day report window can include periods that have already ended; ongoing impacts require verification at the source.

The browser computes distances to event representative points and offers 250, 1,000 and 5,000 km search ranges. These ranges are display filters, not hazard boundaries. Up to four matching reports are shown with official report links. The panel does not place artificial danger circles on the map, route evacuation, or declare a selected place safe. GDACS impact colors describe overall potential humanitarian impact and must not be read as local official weather-warning levels.

No matching retrieved reports does not establish an absence of disasters. Coverage, pagination, event selection, source updates and point-based filtering can omit relevant information. This panel remains separate from the official-warning integrations described above.

## Satellite and base maps

[NASA GIBS](https://nasa-gibs.github.io/gibs-api-docs/access-basics/) publishes the layer metadata and timestamped imagery tiles used by the map:

- `GOES-East_ABI_Band13_Clean_Infrared`
- `GOES-West_ABI_Band13_Clean_Infrared`
- `Himawari_AHI_Band13_Clean_Infrared`
- `MODIS_Terra_CorrectedReflectance_TrueColor`

Infrared geostationary layers represent thermal observations, not visible-color photographs, rainfall radar, or a temperature sensor on the ground. Their viewing geometry has limits: cloud features distort near the limb, and a map zoom beyond native resolution adds no detail. Terra MODIS is a daily global true-color product with daylight, cloud and orbital limitations. It is not a live camera. See [imagery implementation notes](imagery.md) for frame selection and coverage.

Credits belong to NASA GIBS/EOSDIS and the original observing agencies, including NOAA for GOES and JMA for Himawari. A visible satellite layer does not authorize use of agency logos as endorsements.

The base map is [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), with attribution to OpenStreetMap contributors. Use of its public tile service must follow the [OSMF tile policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/). Do not bulk-download tiles or add an offline tile pack. Map coverage and street labels do not establish passable roads or evacuation routes.

## Preparedness references

The original short checklist synthesizes general preparation advice from the [CDC's flood preparedness guide](https://www.cdc.gov/floods/safety/), [Maharashtra SDMA's NDMA do/don't guidance](https://sdma.maharashtra.gov.in/en/dos-and-donts/), and [NWS flood safety resources](https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood). It deliberately leaves location-specific evacuation routes, shelters and telephone numbers to the relevant authority. No source endorses this application.

The self-contained field kit at `/offline.html` also links to [NWS flood actions](https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-during), [NWS lightning guidance](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-tips), [CDC earthquake guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/earthquakes/safety/stay-safe-during-an-earthquake.html), [USFA wildfire evacuation guidance](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/wui/outreach/wildfire-evacuation.html), [NWS hurricane actions](https://www.weather.gov/safety/hurricane-action), [Ready NC communication tips](https://www.readync.gov/stay-informed/tips-communicating), and [NWS wireless-alert information](https://www.weather.gov/wrn/wea). These are short original summaries, not an integrated emergency service. Official source links require internet; the action cards do not.
